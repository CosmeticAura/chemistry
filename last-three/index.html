Last three things

Average atomic mass, the Cr and Cu exceptions, and three facts. About 10 minutes. Read each one, then answer the questions below it and press Enter.

Average atomic mass

Real chlorine is a mix: some atoms weigh 34.969 and some weigh 36.966. The 35.45 on the periodic table is the average weight of one atom from that mix.

Picture 100 chlorine atoms. About 76 weigh 34.969 and about 24 weigh 36.966 (the percents are 75.78% and 24.22%).

  • The 76 light ones: 34.969 × 75.78 = 2650
  • The 24 heavy ones: 36.966 × 24.22 = 895
  • All 100: 2650 + 895 = 3545
  • One atom: 3545 ÷ 100 = 35.45

Dividing by 100 at the end is the same as turning each percent into a decimal first. So:

mass × (percent ÷ 100) for each isotope, then add. Check: the answer lands between the masses, closest to the most common one.

Cr and Cu, the only two exceptions

This is not about charge. It's the normal, neutral atom. Every element follows the filling order except these two.

Cr (24)Cu (29)
The order predicts[Ar] 4s² 3d⁴[Ar] 4s² 3d⁹
The d sublevel isone short of half full (5)one short of full (10)
One 4s electron moves to 3d[Ar] 4s¹ 3d⁵[Ar] 4s¹ 3d¹⁰

Why: a d sublevel that is exactly half full or completely full is extra stable, so the atom gives up one 4s electron to get there. The total doesn't change: 18 + 1 + 5 = 24.

Write this for the explain question: "Copper is [Ar] 4s¹ 3d¹⁰ instead of 4s² 3d⁹ because one 4s electron moves into 3d. A completely full d subshell is more stable."

Charge is a different idea (ions): a positive ion loses its 4s electrons first. Cu⁺ starts from the exception and loses the single 4s: [Ar] 3d¹⁰.

Three facts you missed

Alpha particles are positive (2 protons, 2 neutrons). The nucleus is positive too, and like charges push apart, so the few alpha particles that got close to the tiny nucleus bounced back. If they were negative they'd be pulled in, not bounced back. That was the one wrong part of your gold foil answer.

Thomson found the electron in 1897. His plum pudding model (1904): negative electrons scattered in a positive sphere.

Bohr's model is 1913.
