1. The periodic table is the filling order about 4 points, 8 min

What you're writing. An electron configuration lists where every electron in the atom goes. Each piece, like 2p⁶, has three parts:

  • 2 is the energy level (the row of the periodic table)
  • p is the type of spot: s, p or d
  • ⁶ is the superscript, the small raised number. It's how many electrons are in that spot.

How many each one holds. s holds 2, p holds 6, d holds 10. So 2s² is fine, but 2s⁶ is impossible.

The order you fill them in (the same every time):

Spot1s2s2p3s3p4s3d4p

The bold ones in the box below are where you went wrong on mock test B. You wrote 2s⁶ where it's 2p⁶, 4p where it's 3p, and 3d is the one that comes after 4s even though the number goes down.

How to do one, step by step:

  1. Find the atomic number on the periodic table. That's how many electrons you have to place.
  2. Go through the order above, filling each spot all the way, and keep a running total.
  3. When the next full spot would go past your number, put only the leftover electrons there and stop.
  4. Check: add up all the superscripts. They must equal the atomic number.

Silicon (14): 1s² (total 2), 2s² (4), 2p⁶ (10), 3s² (12), then 3p would be 18, which is too many. 14 − 12 = 2 left, so 3p². Answer: 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p². Check: 2+2+6+2+2 = 14.

Manganese (25): fill up to 4s² (total 20). 3d holds 10 but you only have 25 − 20 = 5 left, so 3d⁵. Answer: 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 4s² 3d⁵.

Why "the periodic table is the order". Each row of the table is one energy level. The two left columns are s spots, the six right columns are p spots, and the ten middle columns (row 4 and down) are d spots. Walk across a row left to right and you're walking through the filling order. Row 4 goes 4s, then the middle block (that's 3d, one number lower), then 4p. So you can read the order straight off the table instead of memorizing it.

Noble gas form is a shortcut. [Ar] stands for the first 18 electrons (1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶), so you start writing at 4s. Mn = [Ar] 4s² 3d⁵.

The order to know: 1s 2s 2p 3s 3p 4s 3d 4p

2. 4s fills first and leaves first about 4 points, 6 min

4s is lower in energy, so it fills before 3d. But 4s is the outer shell (n = 4 is farther out than n = 3), and atoms always lose their outermost electrons first. So a positive ion loses 4s before 3d. Write the neutral atom, then take from 4s.

Q14 answer to memorize: "4s fills before 3d because 4s is lower in energy. But 4s is farther from the nucleus, so when an ion forms, its electrons are removed first."

3. Cr and Cu are exceptions about 3 points, 3 min

A d subshell is more stable exactly half full (d⁵) or completely full (d¹⁰), so one 4s electron moves into 3d. It only happens to Cr and Cu.
